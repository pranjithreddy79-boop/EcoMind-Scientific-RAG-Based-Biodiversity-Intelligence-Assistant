LAND USE KNOWLEDGE

Land Use:
Land use describes how land is managed or utilized, such as forests, grasslands, croplands, plantations, wetlands or urban areas.

Monoculture:
Monoculture is a land-use system dominated by one crop species. It can provide production efficiency but may provide fewer habitat types and resources than more diverse systems.

Land Use and Biodiversity:
Land-use and land-cover change can contribute to the loss of natural ecosystems and biodiversity. The effects depend on the type, intensity and spatial pattern of land management.

Habitat Fragmentation:
Fragmentation occurs when continuous habitat is divided into smaller or more isolated patches. Fragmentation can reduce habitat connectivity and affect movement and access to resources for organisms.

Agroforestry:
Agroforestry integrates trees or other woody perennials with crops and/or animals. Depending on design and local conditions, agroforestry can diversify production, provide habitat, influence microclimate and contribute to ecosystem services.

Vegetation Cover:
Maintaining suitable vegetation cover can influence soil protection, water regulation and habitat availability.

Important Metrics:
- Land-use type
- Habitat diversity
- Habitat connectivity
- Vegetation cover
- Species richness
- Soil organic carbon

Reasoning Relationship:
A monoculture with low habitat diversity may provide fewer habitat types than a diversified land-use system. Where appropriate, integrating suitable vegetation or agroforestry elements can increase structural diversity. The recommendation should consider rainfall, soil moisture, soil carbon and existing biodiversity.
