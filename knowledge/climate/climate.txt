CLIMATE KNOWLEDGE

Temperature:
Temperature affects plant growth, water demand, species distributions and ecosystem processes. Changes in temperature can alter the timing and suitability of habitats for different species.

Rainfall:
Rainfall determines an important part of water availability for terrestrial ecosystems and agriculture. Low or highly variable rainfall can increase water stress for vegetation.

Water Availability and Biodiversity:
Changes in precipitation and temperature can affect freshwater ecosystems and species through changes in water availability, habitat conditions and physiological stress.

Climate and Soil:
Temperature and moisture influence biological activity in soils. Climate conditions can therefore interact with soil organic carbon, vegetation and soil biodiversity.

Climate and Habitat:
Species may respond to changing temperature and precipitation by changing their geographic ranges, seasonal activity or habitat use. Species responses differ between ecosystems and species.

Important Metrics:
- Temperature
- Rainfall
- Soil moisture
- Water availability
- Species richness
- Habitat condition

Reasoning Relationship:
Low rainfall combined with low soil moisture can increase water stress. If vegetation cover is also low, habitat conditions may become less suitable for some species. Climate-related risks should therefore be evaluated together with soil moisture, land use and biodiversity indicators.

Evidence Source:
IPCC AR6 WGII - Chapter 2: Terrestrial and Freshwater Ecosystems and Their Services
IPCC AR6 WGII - Chapter 4: Water
IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Land
